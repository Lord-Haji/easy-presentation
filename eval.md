# Self-Check Rubric (`eval.md`)

Run this checklist before finalizing any slide deck or spoken script.

## Slide Checks
- [ ] **One idea per slide:** Can each slide be summarized in one short headline?
- [ ] **4-second scan:** Does every content slide stay around 25–45 words (excluding chart labels)?
- [ ] **Zero stats soup on slides:** Are dense tables, confidence intervals, and Greek letters kept in the paper/appendix rather than dumped on a 30-second slide?
- [ ] **Method transparency:** Is there one clean, jargon-free visual slide showing how the data was cleaned, checked, and validated?
- [ ] **Visual inspection:** Were all slides exported to PNG and checked for title wrapping, text overflow, or clipped borders?
- [ ] **Clean ending:** Does the deck end on the takeaway slide instead of a filler "Thank you for your attention" slide?

## Spoken Script Checks
- [ ] **Pacing:** Does word count fit 130–145 words per minute for the time slot (e.g., ~400–430 words for 3 minutes)?
- [ ] **10-second hook:** Does the opening motivate the audience to care before listing dataset sizes?
- [ ] **Spoken cadence:** Are sentences 8–16 words with natural contractions and explicit `[pause]`, `[point]`, and `[slow down]` cues?
- [ ] **Coffee test:** Would a real person actually say every sentence out loud?
- [ ] **Zero buzzwords:** Zero `paradigm shift`, `synergy`, `disrupt`, `game changer`, `delve`, or `leverage`.

## Academic Rigor Checks
- [ ] **Verified numbers:** Does every number on every slide and in the script match the raw result files?
- [ ] **Honest limits:** Are key assumptions, who/what labeled the data, null results, and method limitations stated plainly?
- [ ] **Q&A ready:** Are 3–4 short, direct answers prepared for the hardest methodological questions?
